# Vericairn roadmap

This page shows where Vericairn is headed, release by release. Everything after the current published
release is planned, not promised, and has no dates. Each release's notes and documentation set are the
record of what shipped.

## 0.9.4 — current published release

0.9.4 follows 0.9.3 and readies an estate for work that goes on between the commands a person runs. A flow's
step takes its timing, at once, after a delay, at a time, on a schedule, in a window or on an event, and a step may
end in a decision among named paths, made by a rule, an agent in a role or a person in a role; a run that will
never complete is closed with a reason for each step it left unsettled. Agents keep working on a product between
releases as standing agents: a standing goal's triggers make its occurrences due, each carried within its
sponsor's caps, and every possibility, question or publication request a standing agent raises waits for someone
else to adopt, answer or approve it. The duty carrier, a researcher that reads public sources only where its brief
permits, and a bug hunter come as the first standing roles. A run, a timed step and a standing agent's occurrence
are still taken one step per command. Before two assignments run at once in one repository, their permitted paths
are compared, and an estate policy can hold an overlap until it is resolved. When an agent session holding work
goes quiet, whoever is accountable for the goal is asked, a person can hand the step to another holder, and the
displaced holder's late work is refused. People say when they are away, so routing passes over them, and record
quiet hours, which hold notices once notifications arrive. Removing a person's role closes what they may still
write, the operator answers open asks with their recommendations in one signed act and signs every ready item in
one reviewed act, an observer that stops reporting becomes attention, and an estate keeps its progress at a target
its configuration chooses, a GitHub discussion first. An upgrade says whether the release pinned before can still
read the estate, records a restore point and states the way back, and status answers in a few seconds on an estate
of about 8,000 records. 0.9.4 also runs git on a repository it did not create without letting that repository's
configuration start a program, gives a checked push's secret scan only the inputs it writes itself and starts
every program by its resolved absolute path, as the release notes' Security section describes; every
distributable is still built on .NET SDK 10.0.404 and embeds the 10.0.15 runtimes. Everything 0.9.3 brought
remains, with Remote still a developer build you make from source. Its
[release notes](docs/releases/0.9.4/release-notes.md) and [documentation set](docs/releases/0.9.4/README.md)
describe what shipped and its limits.

## 0.9.5 — planned

Remote grows from attention and decisions to supervision: from the phone you steer running work, see the
estate's nodes and connections and hand a task to Workbench; Remote notifies you over the local network,
reminds you before a supervised window opens, handles lost, replaced and shared phones and opens readable
records. From Workbench or the phone, a saved prompt can be sent to a running agent in one step; it steers the
agent without widening what the agent may do. Paired devices reach the runtime from any network through a
relay that forwards bytes it cannot read, and Remote and Workbench say whether a connection is direct or
relayed. MCP clients that sign in only with OAuth work an estate with short-lived tokens.

## 0.9.6 — planned

An estate follows the GitHub conversations it subscribes to and answers only under recorded authority, and it
follows each report it files with another project through to the release that carries the fix. A release's
advisories are drafted from its Security section for you to publish, and every release's bill of materials is
checked against published advisories before acceptance and daily after. You can run Vericairn as an always-on
server for your estates, with every hosted estate keeping a replica you hold. Beside the server, Standard and Pro
carry a run from step to step by themselves, stopping only where a person holds the role, while Community keeps
one step per command.

## 0.10.x — planned

The 0.10.x series begins by completing continuous operation: an operation's work keeps moving through its people
and agents at the times its steps declare, from an always-on server. A reserved act waits for a person unless a
bounded, recorded grant lets a named agent carry it, in the estate's way of working: people only, people and
agents, or agents only. Some acts are never delegated, such as changing who may act or making a legal commitment,
and always wait for a person. The creator domains follow, one release at a time: websites and web applications
with the software foundries and work kept outside git; music production and publishing; creator merchandise and
comics; then books, book series and world building. Each takes the work from drafting through templates and
review to a publishable package with its metadata; you publish on the platform, and Vericairn records the
publication. These domains ship within the Community distributable and move to separate packages at the next
milestone.

## Later milestone: packages, the registry, Knowledge and websites — planned

Extensions install, update and remove as packages, with a registry carrying their trust, and the creator domains
move from the Community distributable into packages. Knowledge holds an estate's articles, terminology and guides,
and Vericairn's websites, including this roadmap, the catalog and the documentation, are generated from the same
records.

## Later milestone: teams and organizations — planned

An organization runs Vericairn for its members on a shared server it operates: many estates, each in its own
account and process, with administration, quotas, monitoring, archiving and upgrades without downtime. Teams work
in the practices they choose, such as Kanban, Scrum or Stage-Gate, on the same records. All of it is Community.

## Editions — planned direction

Community stays free and open source, and no Community feature will move behind payment. Standard is
planned to license the coordinated production features for one person and Pro the same features for teams
and organizations; there are no offers or prices yet. Every paid service Vericairn plans, such as hosting,
a relay for Remote, backups, managed workers or first-tier support by agents, is planned as the same
software you can run yourself at the edition that sells it. You will never be locked in:

1. Every paid service has a self-hosted path at the edition that sells it.
2. No access to your estate requires a Vericairn account; an account only pays for and manages hosted services.
3. An estate moves between Vericairn's hosting and your own in either direction without loss.
4. Paid rights are verified offline, without contacting Vericairn, and outlive its servers.
5. Ending a subscription ends only Vericairn's operation of a service, never self-hosting of covered releases or any Community right.

## Later milestone: paid editions — planned

Once offers are decided, Standard brings coordination of one person's work across machines and Pro the same
across a team, with sign-in through an organization's directory, and support by agents comes with both. Hosting
of an estate's runtime, by Vericairn or a provider you choose, is offered to every edition. Each is software you
can run yourself at the edition that sells it, under the five rules of the Editions section, and no Community
feature moves behind payment.

## Later milestone: specialist workflows — planned

Courses and video, crowdfunding, Unity projects and games follow, with the further foundries, adapters and
providers of the creator domains beyond their first versions. Vericairn's catalog plans more than 3,000 entries
across creative and technical work and 28 industries, listed in the [README](README.md#planned); each further
domain is placed on this roadmap when it is chosen.
