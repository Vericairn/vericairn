# Policy evaluator

Revision 9, October 20, 2026. Describe policy implementations by their compilation identity, result selection per assignment and the boundary for prospective work-state consistency.

The runtime service collects the basis for the [policy read surfaces](policies.md). Application composition installs the foundation kinds, the software pack's reference-coverage kind and the local Git source. A named release preview carries the candidate's exact product, version and commit with preview origin; it cannot authorize dispatch. Source collection reads retained objects and local bytes without fetching or building.

The runtime's internal `Policies` library implements `vericairn.estate-policy/1`. It evaluates supplied snapshots and returns findings. Record validation, source collection, implementation admission and release submission remain responsibilities of its caller. The library adds no command, transport operation or persisted record shape.

`PolicyWork.Results` selects at most one current result from each current assignment's latest attempt at the basis time, following the work-order lineage. Parallel assignments do not compete for one result slot. Equal-time latest attempts within an assignment and multiple current results of its selected attempt remain ambiguous inputs. `PolicyWork.Acceptance` recognizes an exact acceptance of the current order or of any selected result whose assignment pins that exact order revision. Acceptance of an earlier work-order revision remains historical. A newer attempt, superseded assignment or replaced result removes the earlier result from that selection.

The staleness class `result-awaiting-review` excludes accepted work orders before selecting waiting results, just as assignment-start excludes accepted work. All of an accepted order's parallel results are settled for review-wait. Remaining results keep their exact-result review requirement and elapsed-time anchor; acceptance supplies no review evidence.

Work-state consistency warns prospectively from `context.Model.ById[context.Identity].AtTime`, the first declaration of that policy identity. The closed version-1 parameters remain `cases`; no new parameter or status is needed. For an explicit open status and its exact acceptance or publication, the contradiction begins at the later record time. If both records precede the boundary, the case contributes no subject or finding. Equality is included. A new open status can therefore contradict an older acceptance or publication, and a new acceptance or publication can contradict an older open status. `startsAt` still controls activation; edits to it or to the policy do not restart the boundary. Other cases retain their existing satisfied findings, and an empty selection retains the ordinary zero-subject summary.

`PolicyBasis` copies the validated record view, including retained revisions, signatures, diagnostics and supersession links. It discards the loader's filesystem root. Repository inputs name an identity, revision, source contract, completeness and files with normalized relative paths, content hashes and bytes. Retained proof objects carry their hashes and bytes. The basis digest covers these inputs, their availability, the implementation pins and the explicit clock. Canonical output includes diagnostics, evidence, dispositions, counts and ordering.

The caller chooses one clock origin:

| Origin | Contract |
|---|---|
| Recorded | The greatest valid record timestamp in the supplied view; repeated reads do not advance time. |
| Preview | An explicit UTC timestamp with record-format second precision. Earlier replay requires an actual historical snapshot. |
| Submission | An explicit timestamp plus the proposed exact release reference, product, version and commit. The release adapter must collect and recheck these at its submission boundary. |

A timestamp preceding an included record is unavailable. A preview cannot become submission evidence by relabeling its output. The evaluator reads no wall clock, environment, filesystem, process or network. Source adapters perform I/O before constructing the basis. A changed source, time, implementation pin or record view changes the basis.

`PolicyKindRegistry` supplies exact kind/version implementations. Application composition pins a compilation identity: SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string `vericairn.policy-implementation/1|<assembly-name>|<module-version-id>`. The name is the assembly's simple name, and the identifier is its manifest module's deterministic module version identifier in `N` format (32 hexadecimal digits without hyphens). Together with the registered kind and version, this binds the compiled kind, version and module identity that composition admitted. It is not a hash of installed bytes and does not detect a modified installation. The identity is available both in a single-file bundle and in an assembly on disk. Declarations cannot load code or choose an ambient assembly.

The foundation implements staleness, work placement, work-key family, revision-line, work-state consistency, release-entry and recurring-duty version 1. Missing, duplicate or mismatched implementations and unsupported source contracts are unavailable. Kind availability and strict parameters are checked even before activation, while the policy's level waits for `startsAt`. Invalid versioned parameters retain an error diagnostic. Pack conformance compares complete canonical evaluations over explicit vectors, including unavailable and erroneous cases.

Policies match a release to its product through the product record's `supersedes` chain. The release's typed product subject may name any revision of that product record. This rule applies to release-entry cohorts, recurring-duty release schedules and their occurrence evidence and outstanding debt, reference-coverage's recorded-time release selection, release-published obligation gates, the published or withdrawn end of a release placement, and the published-plan-still-open consistency case. A release of another product never matches, even when its version and repository agree. Version strings, publication references and hashes, status checks and timing boundaries remain exact.

Findings keep the policy identity and current revision, stable rule key, target, item, level, owner, basis, clock, underlying verdict, evidence and next action. An exception adds `excepted` only to an unmet finding whose exact policy revision, rule and target match. Authority, bounds, expiry and any exact submission restriction still apply. It changes neither the verdict nor built-in diagnostics. Expiry is exclusive. Multiple matching grants remain listed.

`vericairn.estate/release-entry`, version 1, requires `product`, `entryKind: devlog`, `source: estate-draft/1`, `directory`, `fromPublication` and `review`. The cohort starts inclusively at an exact `{type, id, hash}` publication reference or strictly after the policy identity's first declaration for `after-declaration`, ordered by `(at, id)`. Policy edits do not restart that cohort. Each release lineage contributes its first observed publication, including a release later withdrawn. A candidate withdrawn before publication contributes none.

The source is a complete estate repository snapshot plus its document pins and retained first revision bytes. Estate and matching product selections use that estate repository; a repository selection must name it and cover the entire draft directory with `**` or `directory/**`. Missing history, source bytes, conflicting paths or malformed metadata make the rule unavailable. The source is bounded at 10,000 Markdown files, 32 MiB each of current and retained first-revision draft bytes, 1 MiB per parsed draft, 10,000 data nodes and depth 16. No partial count is reported.

The `estate-draft/1` reader accepts a deliberately bounded YAML data subset between `---` delimiters: block and flow mappings/sequences, plain strings, single-quoted strings, double-quoted strings with JSON escapes, and literal/folded block strings. Duplicate keys, merge keys, tags, anchors, aliases, implicit null/boolean/numeric values and unsupported types are unavailable. Quote ambiguous strings. Metadata declares `kind`, `id`, `title`, `summary`, `audience`, `applicability`, `sources` and `status`; optional fields are `release`, `sites`, `event`, `drafted-by`, `supersedes` and `guidance`. Applicability has a string `versions` and optional product display string. A release relation has exact `product`, `version` and `publication: {id, hash}` (optional `type: release`). An exact source uses `{type, id, hash}`, with optional `tracking: historical` or `tracked`; a source string alone cannot prove a publication reference.

To qualify, first and current bytes must preserve the stable entry id and release relation, and the first pin must follow the publication. The current bytes need a valid document pin, nonempty title, summary and body, `audience: internal`, `status: draft`, exact `applicability.versions`, and the publication in `sources`. The latest current ordinary review of that exact pin, ordered by `(at, id)`, must pass at the declared `independent` or `operator` independence. A later adverse qualifying review removes satisfaction; a content correction needs its own review. Living-document clearances do not count. Editorial quality remains the reviewer's judgment.

Each publication has one `entry` finding. Exactly one qualifying identity and path satisfies it; missing entries and duplicates remain named. Copies of an old identity cannot acquire new authorship through a new path or pin. At refuse, unresolved publications hold later submissions for that product, while a candidate owes no account of its own future publication. The runtime's policy surfaces and release gate use this evaluator; first-party declarations remain subject to POLICY-04's exact adoption approval.

`WritingEvaluation` shares the finite spacing scanner with the existing repository check. It scans supplied Markdown and the record format's prose fields. Exact, case-sensitive tokens and union scopes select occurrences; no diagnostic prose is parsed to obtain a token. Matched occurrences remain listed with every matching entry and leave the warning count once. Unused active entries warn only after the entire scan completes. A missing source leaves match counts unknown. Inline code is blanked without shifting offsets; fenced blocks use the scanner's explicit opening and closing grammar. Locations include original UTF-8 byte offsets and one-based lines.

Scanner contract `vericairn.writing-spacing/3` uses the same current declaration, repository and path ownership lookup as generator verification. An unretired `kind: generated` declaration excludes its whole path or bounded pattern members from spacing scans. An authored declaration excludes only the content of its declared generated regions with valid, unique, closed and unnested markers. Undeclared or malformed markers grant no exclusion. Generated region fences do not change parsing of the surrounding authored lines, whose locations retain their original bytes and line numbers. Record prose fields remain scanned even when their text also appears in a generated copy. These exclusions do not count as writing exceptions or weaken generator verification and other content checks.

Recurring duties reconstruct occurrences from all retained policy revisions. Day intervals use 86,400 seconds; months use the original UTC Gregorian anchor with month-end clamping. Publication schedules use the first observed publication of each release identity. Dated duties retain their exact adopted date evidence, including an already-due date at adoption. A changed behavioral definition replaces only unopened future occurrences. Old debt retains its original subject, dates, grace and proof requirements, while the current owner and level apply.

Each due occurrence remains individually represented. During grace it is informational and unfinished. At the deadline unfinished work is unmet; a completion exactly at the deadline is on time. Late completion keeps its missed deadline and lateness. Approved `ended-unmet` dispositions retain the debt as historical unmet work and never claim completion. Removed or retired duties retain their history without active enforcement.

Completion requires an exact result binding, every required check once and passed, authentic signatures, the declared native action proofs, available retained bytes and the required exact-result review. A result verification selects the latest current verification of the exact result and method; the review must read that selected proof. Native action timestamps and their first qualifying lineage events prevent editorial revisions from prepaying work. Repository verification pins the declared commit source. Continuing date renewals also require the first following behavioral revision to declare a qualifying next date with new source evidence.

Proof replay retains continuous qualification periods. A later affirmative review preserves the first completion time. An adverse review, withdrawn proof or superseded result ends that period; recovery has a new completion time. Verification expiry is checked when a period starts, while loss of required source access makes evaluation unavailable. Separate result bindings can discharge several already-due occurrences; they do not imply coverage of omitted or future work.

The current implementation bounds a kind at 100,000 records, recurring-duty expansion at 10,000 findings including its next scheduled occurrence, and duty proof replay at 1,000,000 steps per rule. Exhaustion or calendar overflow returns an unavailable rule, never a truncated successful inventory. These bounds, source requirements and implementation pins are part of the evaluation contract and must be preserved or explicitly revised by application composition.

The software pack supplies `vericairn.software/reference-coverage`, version 1, through its internal registration factory. Its closed parameters are `product`, `repository`, `referenceManifest`, `registerRepository`, `registerPath` and a nonempty unique subset of `command`, `record-type`, `api-operation` in `categories`. The repository must have an explicit product relation through its subject, the product's changes declaration, or the matching repository in its GitHub release destination. A repository selection covers the register; a product selection must name the same product. Missing implementations or sources remain unavailable at the policy's declared level. The proposed first-party declaration uses warn.

The reader verifies the entire [reference inventory](reference-inventory.md) before counting any selected category. It then parses the register's single **Generated reference coverage** table with the exact columns `Category`, `Reference`, `Term`, and its terminology tables with `Term`, `Meaning`, `Not to be confused with`, `Owning document and section`, `Status`. It handles escaped punctuation, cell separators and matching backtick runs, without treating fenced examples as tables or normalizing identity case or internal whitespace. A malformed selected table, duplicate coverage table or identical duplicate mapping row makes the rule unavailable. Conflicting mappings, duplicate current term names, missing or noncurrent terms, empty definitions, and absent owning document/section text fail the affected inventory items. Owning references must name a relative Markdown document plus a named or numbered section; this structural check does not replace reviewing that owning section's content.

Each missing or invalid inventory identity has one finding keyed by category and exact name, sorted in that order. Several identities may refer to one concept. Mappings absent from the selected build are informational historical coverage and never cover another item. The register is bounded at 16 MiB and 100,000 total terminology and mapping rows; the kind allows at most 100,001 findings. Unavailable inputs yield no partial count. An unavailable warning remains visible and does not hold a release.

At recorded time, the basis copies and hashes the estate manifest as well as the records. Coverage resolves the pinned product release's observed publication to its exact commit. A submission uses its explicitly supplied candidate commit; a preview uses the explicitly supplied source revision and retains its preview origin. The selected inventory and every generated reference must match that commit. The adapter retains the reference-set members under the declaration's relative manifest path in the product repository's source namespace. It must collect those exact bytes before evaluation; the kind never checks out a branch, executes generation or fetches missing content. POLICY-03 supplies the application adapters and surface integration, and POLICY-04 adopts exact first-party definitions.
