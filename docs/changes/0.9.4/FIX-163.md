# FIX-163: A release basis past the object limit is retained as a split object

## docs/operator-guide.md

### In "Release procedure", after the paragraph beginning "From 0.9.2 (FIX-95), a basis keeps the files of a repository"

> From 0.9.4 (FIX-163), a basis larger than the estate's 8 MiB object limit, as an estate with many thousands of records
> produces, is retained in parts of exactly 8 MiB, the last no longer, named in order by a small canonical root object of
> format `vericairn.split-object/1` that records the whole basis's length and SHA-256; the evidence names the root, and
> `release basis <evidence-hash>` reads it exactly as a basis held in one object. A basis is at most 256 MiB, 32 parts, and
> `release submit --check` refuses a longer one with `release.basis-size`. A part that is missing, of the wrong length or
> changed is named as `basis-part:<n>@<hash>`. Commit the parts and the root with the basis's other objects: the estate
> check reports `objects.split` for a root committed without a part it names. A basis that fits one object is retained as
> that object, as before. A client before 0.9.4 reports a split basis as
> `No supported retained release basis has this hash`, and its estate check passes; read the basis with 0.9.4 or later.

## docs/limitations-and-platforms.md

### In "Workbench, MCP and living documents", after the paragraph beginning "POLICY-03 combines built-in objections"

> From 0.9.4 (FIX-163), a basis that still outgrows the 8 MiB object limit, as one naming the records of an estate with many
> thousands of them does, is no longer refused: it is retained in parts of the limit, named in order by a small root object
> with the whole basis's length and SHA-256, and reads as before. A basis is at most 256 MiB (32 parts); a longer one is
> refused with `release.basis-size` before anything is written. Its reader refuses a root past that bound, or naming parts
> that do not follow from its length, before it reads any part. A client before 0.9.4 reports such a basis as unsupported.

## docs/api-v1.md

### In "Release-basis diagnostics (0.8.3)", after the paragraph beginning "From 0.9.2 (FIX-95), retained bases have format"

> From 0.9.4 (FIX-163), a basis whose serialization passes the 8 MiB object limit is retained as a split object: parts of
> exactly 8 MiB, the last no longer, and a canonical root of format `vericairn.split-object/1` naming them in order with the
> basis's length and SHA-256. `GET /v1/release-bases/{hash}` with the root's hash returns the same `ReleaseBasisResponse` as
> for the basis held in one object, its `hash` the root's and its evidence of format `vericairn.release-basis/3`. Before it
> reads any part, the reader refuses an object past the object limit, a root larger than any root of a basis within 256 MiB,
> stating more than 256 MiB, naming a count of parts that does not follow from its length, or not canonical, each as
> `release-basis.invalid` at `basis:<hash>`, and it measures every part: one missing is `release-basis.missing` and one of
> another length than its place holds is `release-basis.invalid`, at `basis-part:<n>@<hash>`. Read parts whose bytes do not
> match their hash are `release-basis.hash-mismatch` at their place, and parts that join to other bytes than the root's
> digest are `release-basis.hash-mismatch` at `basis:<digest>`. A runtime before 0.9.4 answers HTTP 404 for a root.

## docs/api-compatibility-0.9.4.md

### A new section, "A release basis past the object limit is retained as a split object (FIX-163)"

> `Vericairn.Records` adds the content-addressed split object in `Vericairn.Records.Estate`: the static class `SplitObject`
> (`Format`, `vericairn.split-object/1`; `MaxRootBytes`, `Plan`, `Write`, `IsRoot`, `Read` and `Join`), the interface
> `ISplitObjectSource`, which measures an object before it is read, the records `SplitObjectPlan`, `SplitObjectContent`
> (with `Split`) and `SplitObjectProblem`, and the enum `SplitObjectFault`. These are additions; no member changes. A reader
> bounds the root, honors its caller's maximum, ties the count to the length and measures every part before it reads any.
> Release submission retains a basis past the object limit as a split object, up to 256 MiB, and names its root in the
> operation's evidence; `release.basis-size` refuses a longer basis. The release-basis read returns the response it gives for
> the basis held in one object, under the root's hash. The estate check adds `objects.split` for a canonical root whose parts
> are missing, of the wrong length or bytes. No record field, record type or format number changes, and
> `vericairn.release-basis/3` is unchanged. A 0.9.3 estate check passes an estate holding split objects; 0.9.3's
> `release basis` reports a root as `No supported retained release basis has this hash` and exits 2, its API answers HTTP 404
> and its SDK null.
