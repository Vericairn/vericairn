# FIX-163: A release basis past the object limit is retained as a split object

## docs/operator-guide.md

### In "Release procedure", after the paragraph beginning "From 0.9.2 (FIX-95), a basis keeps the files of a repository"

> From 0.9.4 (FIX-163), a basis larger than the estate's 8 MiB object limit, as an estate with many thousands of records
> produces, is retained in parts of at most 8 MiB each, named in order by a small root object of format
> `vericairn.split-object/1` that records the whole basis's length and SHA-256; the evidence names the root, and
> `release basis <evidence-hash>` reads it exactly as a basis held in one object. A part that is missing or changed is named
> as `basis-part:<n>@<hash>`, and commit the parts and the root with the basis's other objects. A basis that fits one object
> is retained as that object, as before. A client before 0.9.4 reports a split basis as
> `No supported retained release basis has this hash`, and its estate check passes; read the basis with 0.9.4 or later.

## docs/limitations-and-platforms.md

### In "Workbench, MCP and living documents", after the paragraph beginning "POLICY-03 combines built-in objections"

> From 0.9.4 (FIX-163), a basis that still outgrows the 8 MiB object limit, as one naming the records of an estate with many
> thousands of them does, is no longer refused: it is retained in parts each within the limit, named in order by a small root
> object with the whole basis's length and SHA-256, and reads as before. Only a basis whose root would itself pass the limit,
> more than about 113,000 parts, is refused, before anything is written. A client before 0.9.4 reports such a basis as
> unsupported.

## docs/api-v1.md

### In "Release-basis diagnostics (0.8.3)", after the paragraph beginning "From 0.9.2 (FIX-95), retained bases have format"

> From 0.9.4 (FIX-163), a basis whose serialization passes the 8 MiB object limit is retained as a split object: parts of at
> most 8 MiB and a root of format `vericairn.split-object/1` naming them in order with the basis's length and SHA-256.
> `GET /v1/release-bases/{hash}` with the root's hash returns the same `ReleaseBasisResponse` as for the basis held in one
> object, its `hash` the root's and its evidence of format `vericairn.release-basis/3`. A part missing or changed is
> `release-basis.missing` or `release-basis.hash-mismatch` at `basis-part:<n>@<hash>`, a part whose length breaks the layout
> is `release-basis.invalid` there, and parts that join to other bytes than the root's length and digest are
> `release-basis.hash-mismatch` at `basis:<digest>`. A runtime before 0.9.4 answers HTTP 404 for a root.

## docs/api-compatibility-0.9.4.md

### A new section, "A release basis past the object limit is retained as a split object (FIX-163)"

> `Vericairn.Records` adds `SplitObject` in `Vericairn.Records.Estate`, the content-addressed split object: `Format`
> (`vericairn.split-object/1`), `Plan`, `Write`, `IsRoot`, `Read` and `Join`, with the records `SplitObjectPlan`,
> `SplitObjectContent` and `SplitObjectProblem` and the enum `SplitObjectFault`. These are additions; no member changes.
> Release submission retains a basis past the object limit as a split object and names its root in the operation's evidence,
> and `release.basis-size` judges each object it would write, refusing only a basis whose root would pass the limit. The
> release-basis read returns the response it gives for the basis held in one object, under the root's hash. No record field,
> record type or format number changes, and `vericairn.release-basis/3` is unchanged. A 0.9.3 estate check passes an estate
> holding split objects; 0.9.3's `release basis` reports a root as `No supported retained release basis has this hash` and
> exits 2, its API answers HTTP 404 and its SDK null.
