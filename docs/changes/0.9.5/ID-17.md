# ID-17: A device's signatures and revocation reach

## docs/operator-guide.md

### In "3. Principals, devices and admission", after the paragraph beginning "`devices create` generates the key"

> From 0.9.5 (ID-17), run `vericairn devices reach <device> --since <time>` to inspect available signatures by a suspected device, including superseded records and earlier keys. The cutoff is inclusive. The record's stated time comes from its author. The presence ledger gives this machine's logged signing time and the session's announced list where available. A checkpoint's stated time dates its witness of the exact id and hash. A first witness at or after the cutoff includes a backdated record. A signed device closing at or after the cutoff also includes signatures outside the closing's witnessed history, which can include older records arriving from another copy. Neither checkpoint time establishes the exact signing time.
>
> `devices revoke` reports the writing runtime process and the server that handled the write, where applicable. `devices reach` shows revocation records in this copy and retained observations from this machine's daemon journal, using the same journal epoch to identify the server. A revocation's stated record and effective times come from its author. Observation times come from the observer's clock. Absence of an observation leaves that observer's status unknown. Run the command against other copies for evidence kept there. See [Devices reach](cli.md#vericairn-devices-reach-095) for the JSON form.

## docs/api-v1.md

### After the routes table entry for `POST /v1/devices/revoke`

> From 0.9.5 (ID-17), the revocation's write outcome may also carry `revocationReach`. Each entry gives the observer's `kind` (`runtime` or `server`), `identity`, own clock time `at` and `evidence`. The writing process identifies itself with `written`. A daemon that handled the API write identifies its journal epoch with `write-served`, matching the identity used for retained observations from that journal. The evidence refers to the outcome's exact `written` record.
