# ID-17: A device's signatures and revocation reach

## docs/operator-guide.md

### In "3. Principals, devices and admission", after the paragraph beginning "`devices create` generates the key"

> From 0.9.5 (ID-17), run `vericairn devices reach <device> --since <time>` to inspect available signatures by a suspected device, including superseded records and earlier keys. The cutoff is inclusive. A record is listed unless a counting checkpoint signed by another device witnesses its exact id and hash before the cutoff. A checkpoint can witness the version in its own inventory or through checkpoints it follows by hash. Checkpoints signed by any revision of the suspected device supply no cutoff evidence.
>
> The text and JSON reports label each row by its evidence. **Witnessed after the cutoff** (`witnessed-after-cutoff`) means the first qualifying checkpoint is dated at or after the cutoff. **Unwitnessed** (`unwitnessed`) includes a backdated record with no qualifying checkpoint, or a row drawn only from the presence ledger with no qualifying witness. **Listed on the record's own claimed time** (`claimed-time`) means the record claims a time at or after the cutoff and has no qualifying witness. **Signed after the cutoff by this machine's ledger** (`ledger-time`) means an independent checkpoint witnessed the content before the cutoff, but this machine logged the device's signing at or after it; a checkpoint pins content, not signatures. The record and checkpoint times are their authors' claims. The presence ledger gives this machine's logged signing time and the session's announced list where available. The report also shows when a record is outside the witnessed history of a signed device closing dated at or after the cutoff. Older records can arrive from another copy after a closing.
>
> `devices revoke` reports the writing runtime process and the server that handled the write, where applicable. `devices reach` asks a serving daemon for the revocations it sees and for its observations. A live answer identifies the daemon by its current journal epoch, even when the journal has no retained event for that record. Without a daemon, the embedded runtime reads this copy and the local journal snapshot. A revocation's stated record and effective times come from its author. Observation times come from the observer's clock. Absence of an observation leaves that observer's status unknown. An authorized client can query another serving daemon through the API or SDK. See [Devices reach](cli.md#vericairn-devices-reach-095) for the JSON form.

### Before "Changes visible to scripts"

> ## Revocation reach (0.9.5)
>
> From 0.9.5 (ID-17), the revocation's write outcome may also carry `revocationReach`. Each entry gives the observer's `kind`
> (`runtime` or `server`), `identity`, own clock time `at` and `evidence`. The writing process identifies itself with `written`.
> A daemon that handled the API write identifies its journal epoch with `write-served`, matching the identity used for retained
> observations from that journal. The evidence refers to the outcome's exact `written` record.
>
> `GET /v1/devices/{id}/revocation-reach` reports what the responding daemon observes for a device identity or revision id.
> The local socket uses its operating-system user boundary. A network client signs as an operator or a principal with
> `administer` scope. The response has `device` (the stable device id), `revocations` and an optional `journalProblem`.
> An unknown device returns `404`. Each revocation has `id`, `hash`, the record's claimed `at` and `revokedAt`, `counts`,
> `current` and `observations`. For each exact version it reads, the daemon reports `record-read` for its process and
> `server-read` for its current journal epoch, plus `journal-found` or `journal-written` for matching retained events.
> Use `counts` and `current` to judge the record's standing. The API read writes nothing. The SDK exposes
> `DeviceRevocationReach` and `DeviceRevocationReachAsync`.

## docs/api-compatibility-0.9.5.md

### Under the 0.9.5 changes

> The additive ID-17 `GET /v1/devices/{id}/revocation-reach` read and the SDK's `DeviceRevocationReach` and `DeviceRevocationReachAsync` report exact revocation observations from the responding runtime and its journal. The signed network read requires an operator or `administer` authority.
